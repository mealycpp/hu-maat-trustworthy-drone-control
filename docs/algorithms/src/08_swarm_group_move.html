1. Purpose and context

An operator command such as "All move forward 1 meter" or "Horus, Ra and Thoth go back 40 centimeters" moves a group as a rigid body. Because all members receive an identical translation, their mutual distances are unchanged in the ideal model, so the formation keeps its shape and intra-group conflicts cannot arise from the plan itself. Direction words are interpreted in the arena frame (the yaw-0 heading), not in each aircraft's body frame, so that aircraft with different headings still move in parallel. (A single named aircraft is moved in its body frame instead, Algorithm 7.2.)

2. Inputs and outputs

Input: targets (|targets| > 1, from Algorithm 3 with operator confirmation), parameters among dx, dy, dz, forward_m, right_m (metres). Output: goal map {name: (x, y, z)}; the supervisor sets each target and mode MOVE; arrival returns each member to HOVER (Algorithm 9).

3. Algorithm

Algorithm 8.1 PLAN-GROUP-MOVE
Source: festival/control_server_hu.py: Fleet._plan_group_move (caller Fleet.command holds the lock)
1function PLAN-GROUP-MOVE(targets, params)
2 read dx, dy, dz, fwd, right (default 0); require all finite and each |·| ≤ 2.0
3 Δ ← (dx + fwd, dy − right, dz) // arena ENU: forward = +x, right = −y
4 M ← members; O ← fleet \ M
5 for d in M do
6 require not d.fault
7 require d.mode ∈ {HOVER, FORMATION}
8 require ‖p_d − target_d‖ ≤ 0.12 and ‖v_d‖ ≤ 0.12 // settled
9 for o in O do
10 require o.mode ∉ {MOVE, FORMATION, TAKEOFF, LANDING, SPIN, TURN}
11 require fresh(o)
12 for each pair a, b in M do require ‖p_a − p_b‖ ≥ 0.88
13 for d in M do
14 g_d ← p_d + Δ
15 require |g_d.x|, |g_d.y| ≤ 3.35 and 0.65 ≤ g_d.z ≤ 2.8 // refused, never clamped
16 for o in O, for k = 0..50 do
17 if ‖p_d + Δ·k/50 − p_o‖ < 1.0 then refuse "would pass within 1.0 m of o"
18 return { d: g_d }

Members' freshness is checked earlier in Fleet.command (risk-increasing actions with stale targets fail closed), and the MOVE-family precondition requires each member to be airborne.

6. Complexity

O(|M|² + 51·|M|·|O|); at most 51·6 = 306 distance checks for N = 5.

7. Properties

Guaranteed

  • All members get the identical displacement; planned pairwise distances inside the group are unchanged (shape preserved in the setpoint model).
  • No partial or clamped execution: any violated condition refuses the whole group before any setpoint changes.
  • Non-members must be stationary (not in a manoeuvring or landing mode) and fresh; each member's straight path is sampled against them at 1.0 m.
  • Arena-frame semantics: "right" is −y for every member regardless of its yaw.

Not guaranteed

  • The controller tracks each member independently (per-axis velocity limits, PD law), so members do not move in lock-step; intra-group spacing can vary transiently. Only the watchdog (0.82 m) protects during the move.
  • Members start from their current positions, not their nominal slots; small residual slot errors carry over.
  • Clearance to non-members is sampled (1/50 of the leg) and assumes they remain where they are.
  • The arena-frame convention differs from the body-frame convention for a single aircraft; the operator must know whether one or several aircraft are addressed.
  • A member that started in FORMATION ends the move in HOVER (MOVE always completes to HOVER), so a later step that expects FORMATION mode must re-issue the formation.

9. Comparison with alternatives and related work

Formation control is commonly classified as virtual-structure, leader–follower or consensus/behaviour-based, or, by what agents sense, as position-, displacement- or distance-based [4]. The HU-MA'AT group move is a position-based, open-loop instance of the virtual-structure idea: a rigid displacement is applied once to all setpoints in the global (arena) frame, and each aircraft then tracks its own setpoint with no feedback from the others.

ApproachMechanismWhat it guarantees (under its assumptions)Compared with ours
Virtual structure [1]The formation is a rigid body; the structure is moved, each robot tracks its point in it, and robot errors are fed back to the motion of the structure.High-precision shape keeping; with feedback the structure slows down when a robot lags.Ours applies the rigid displacement once and has no feedback from members to the structure: a lagging member is not waited for.
Leader–follower [2]Followers control distance and bearing (or two distances) to a leader or neighbours along a control graph.Stability of the shape variables for the modelled (nonholonomic) robots; needs only relative sensing.Ours needs global positions from the simulator and has no leader; a single aircraft's failure does not propagate, but it is also not compensated.
Consensus-based [3]Each agent updates its state from neighbours over a communication graph; formation as consensus on offsets.Convergence under connectivity conditions; robustness to link changes and delays analysed.Ours is centralized in one process and has no communication model.
HU-MA'AT group move (this document)Identical arena-frame displacement for every member, admitted only if settled, spaced ≥ 0.88 m, in bounds, and clear of stationary non-members by 1.0 m.Properties of Section 7: shape preserved in the setpoint model; all-or-nothing admission.—

Where ours is weaker. No inter-member coupling during the move (shape is kept only because the controllers happen to behave alike: measured shape change ≤ 0.012 m in the swarm test), no analysis of transient spacing, dependence on global positioning and on a single central process, and no avoidance of moving non-members beyond the mode rule. Where it is adequate. Short (≤ 2 m) translations of a settled group in an open simulated arena, where the transparency of a single displacement vector and the strict admission checks matter more than tracking performance.
