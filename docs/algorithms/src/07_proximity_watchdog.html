1. Purpose and context

All planning in the demo (Algorithms 5, 6, 8) is done on idealised straight-line paths. The proximity watchdog is the runtime backstop in the supervisor's 10 Hz control loop: it observes actual odometry and stops a manoeuvring aircraft that gets too close to another aircraft. Around it are a set of mode rules that keep parked aircraft disarmed, prevent "hidden" take-offs from hold or fault-clear commands, and gate single-aircraft relative moves with a static path check.

2. Inputs, outputs, state

Per tick inputs: for each aircraft, odometry position/velocity/yaw with receive time, current mode, setpoint, fault flag. Outputs: velocity commands on /<name>/gazebo/command/twist, motor enable/disable on /<name>/enable, mode changes and events (SEPARATION_HOLD, LANDED). Modes: parked = {READY, GROUNDED, WAITING, QUEUED}; manoeuvring = {TAKEOFF, MOVE, FORMATION, TURN, SPIN}; others HOVER, LANDING, FAULT.

6. Complexity

Watchdog: O(N²) distance checks per tick (N = 5: 20). Move admission: O(51·N).

7. Properties

Guaranteed

  • Whenever a fresh manoeuvring aircraft (FORMATION/MOVE/TAKEOFF) is observed within 0.82 m of a fresh peer at a tick, its setpoint is frozen at its current position within that tick and it leaves the manoeuvring mode (it will not resume the old goal by itself).
  • Landings are never interrupted by a proximity hold; emergency landings of close aircraft proceed.
  • An aircraft below 0.30 m is never reported as HOVER by a hold; it is disarmed and reported GROUNDED.
  • Parked aircraft receive an explicit disarm every tick, so a just-spawned model held by the velocity controller at its spawn height is not left hovering while reported as parked.
  • Hold, land and fault-clear never launch a parked aircraft; a faulted aircraft accepts only land, clear_fault, or the emergency hover.
  • A single relative move is admitted only from HOVER, with the rest of the fleet not manoeuvring, and with every sampled straight-line point ≥ 1.0 m from every other aircraft's current position.

Not guaranteed

  • Not a collision-avoidance guarantee. Freezing the setpoint does not stop the aircraft instantly; with velocities up to 0.62 m/s per horizontal axis, distance can still shrink after detection. Both aircraft are not necessarily held: only aircraft in the listed modes are; a HOVER, TURN or SPIN aircraft is never held, and a HOVER peer does not move away.
  • Detection is sampled at 10 Hz on odometry; a close pass between ticks is not seen.
  • Aircraft with stale telemetry are invisible to the watchdog (excluded from C) and receive zero velocity with motors not re-enabled; their real position is unknown.
  • A held aircraft is not re-planned; the operator or Algorithm 11 must decide the next step.
  • Path admission uses other aircraft's current positions only (assumed stationary); concurrent manoeuvres are prevented by the mode rule, not by prediction.
  • Measured: after holds, the observed separation continued to shrink to 0.633 m and 0.551 m (Section 8).

9. Comparison with alternatives and related work

Runtime inter-robot collision avoidance is usually done by changing each robot's velocity command continuously, either geometrically (velocity obstacles and their reciprocal variant ORCA) or by a safety filter over a dynamics model (control barrier functions solved as a quadratic program). The HU-MA'AT watchdog is not an avoidance controller: it is a threshold monitor that stops the setpoint motion of the offending aircraft and hands the decision back to the operator or mission coordinator.

ApproachMechanismWhat it guarantees (under its assumptions)Compared with ours
Velocity obstacles [1]Choose a velocity outside the cone of velocities that would collide with each obstacle within a horizon.No collision with obstacles that keep their current velocity; feasibility is enforced through the admissible velocities set by acceleration limits.Ours does not use velocities of peers and does not choose an avoiding velocity; it only freezes the setpoint.
ORCA [2]Each agent takes half of the responsibility for avoiding each neighbour; one low-dimensional linear program per agent and cycle, no communication.Collision-free motion for holonomic agents when all agents run ORCA.Ours is not reciprocal: only an aircraft in FORMATION, MOVE or TAKEOFF reacts; a HOVER, TURN or SPIN peer does nothing.
Control barrier functions, QP safety filter [3]; multi-robot safety barrier certificates [4]Minimally modify a nominal controller so that a barrier function of the pairwise distance stays non-negative; centralized or decentralized QP each cycle.Forward invariance of the safe set for the modelled dynamics and input bounds (formal guarantee within the model).Ours has no dynamics model and no notion of braking distance; it reacts only after the distance is already below 0.82 m.
HU-MA'AT proximity watchdog (this document)3D distance below 0.82 m at a 10 Hz tick ⇒ freeze setpoint, zero twist, mode HOVER (or GROUNDED below 0.30 m), event.Only the properties of Section 7; no collision-avoidance guarantee.—

Where ours is weaker. On every safety property the three established methods establish under their assumptions, ours offers nothing comparable: it is reactive rather than predictive, unilateral rather than reciprocal, and blind between ticks. The live run of 2026-11-12 (Section 8) shows the consequence directly: after the holds the observed separation still fell to 0.633 m and 0.551 m, well below the 0.82 m hold distance. What it does provide. It acts on observed odometry rather than on predicted plans, it never interrupts a landing, its trigger and effect are logged as a single event, and it is small enough to review line by line. The current mitigation is upstream (larger planned clearance), not a better runtime law.

10. Relation to other algorithms

Runtime backstop for Algorithms 5, 6 and 8; tick structure shared with Algorithm 9. Algorithm 11 detects a hold indirectly (live setpoint deviating from the checked plan) and stops the mission. The 0.82 m value is also the veto reference in Algorithms 14 and 18 and the alert reference in Algorithm 17.
